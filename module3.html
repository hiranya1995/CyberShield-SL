🌐 Visual Breakdown: Safe Browsing & Protecting Government PC Devices

HTTPS Padlock Icon 🔒

Before logging into any portal or web system at work, ensure the web address begins with https:// and shows a locked padlock icon. This encrypts your connection.

2

Fake Web Addresses vs Real Official Domains

Official Sri Lankan government sites end in .gov.lk (e.g. gampaha.dist.gov.lk). Watch out for fake typo clones like gampaha-gov-lk.com or e-office-g0v.lk!

3

USB Pen Drive Safety

Plugging an unknown USB flash drive into a government computer can instantly infect the network with ransomware! Always scan USB drives with official antivirus software first.

4

Unsafe Public Wi-Fi Networks

Public Wi-Fi networks at cafes, stations, or airports are unencrypted. Hackers on the same network can intercept your government emails. Always connect via official government VPN.

✅ What Government Staff SHOULD Do

  • Always check that the address bar shows https:// and .gov.lk.
  • Scan all USB drives brought by visitors or staff before opening files.
  • Use official government VPN when working remotely or outside the office.

❌ What Government Staff Should NEVER Do

  • Never plug random USB drives found in parking lots or waiting areas into office PCs.
  • Never download free pirated software or unapproved games onto government machines.
  • Never ignore Windows antivirus warning popups or disable security software.
What is Malware?

Malware (malicious software) is any program or code designed to harm a computer system, steal data, or gain unauthorized access. It is one of the most serious cybersecurity threats facing Sri Lankan government institutions today.

Government Systems at Risk

A malware infection on a government computer can compromise millions of citizen records, disrupt public services, and cause irreparable damage to national security. Prevention is always better than recovery.

Types of Malware

Ransomware

Encrypts your files and demands payment to restore access. Devastating for government data.

Trojan Horse

Disguises itself as legitimate software to trick users into installing it.

Spyware

Silently records keystrokes, screen activity, and transmits data to attackers.

Worm

Self-replicates across a network, consuming bandwidth and crashing systems.

Adware

Bombards users with unwanted ads and can redirect browsing to malicious sites.

Rootkit

Hides deeply in the OS, giving attackers long-term, hidden control over a system.

How Malware Spreads
  • Email Attachments: Malicious files disguised as invoices, reports, or government forms.
  • USB / External Drives: Infected flash drives left in public areas — never plug in unknown drives.
  • Malicious Downloads: Software downloaded from unofficial or torrent websites.
  • Drive-by Downloads: Visiting a compromised website automatically downloads malware.
  • Network Propagation: Worms spread automatically through shared network folders.
Prevention Best Practices
Keep software updated Use antivirus software Never use unknown USB drives Back up data regularly No unofficial software Report infections immediately
