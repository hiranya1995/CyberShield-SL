🔍 Visual Breakdown: How to Spot Fake Emails at Your Office Desk
1

Fake Sender Address (Spoofed Domain)

Real government emails always end in official domains like @dist.gov.lk. Fake emails use tricks like government.support@tax-refunds.net or admin@gov-lk-update.com to trick officers.

2

Urgent Threat Language

Phrases like "ACT NOW OR ACCOUNT TERMINATED IN 2 HOURS" create panic. Attackers want you to act fast without checking with your IT officer.

3

Generic Salutations

Instead of your actual name or official designation, scam emails say "Dear Valued Citizen" or "Dear Sir/Madam".

4

Malicious Links & Attachments

Hovering over the link reveals non-government websites (e.g. fake.gov.portal.com). Never open unexpected .exe or zip files!

✅ What Government Staff SHOULD Do

  • Verify the sender's full email address (check the part after @).
  • Hover over links to inspect the actual destination URL before clicking.
  • Report suspicious emails immediately to your divisional IT officer.

❌ What Government Staff Should NEVER Do

  • Never enter government system passwords on external websites.
  • Never open unexpected email attachments or macro-enabled files.
  • Never reply to panic-inducing emails asking for NIC or OTP details.
What is Phishing?

Phishing is a deceptive email or message attack where criminals pretend to be official government departments, banks, or colleagues to trick you into giving away passwords, citizen identity numbers, or clicking malicious links.

IMPORTANT FOR GOVERNMENT OFFICERS:

Over 90% of security breaches start with a single clicked link in a phishing email. Staying alert protects government servers and citizen confidentiality!

4 Common Types of Phishing

1. Mass Email Phishing

Fake circulars or tax/bonus notices sent to hundreds of officers hoping someone clicks.

2. Targeted Spear Phishing

Customized scam emails using your exact name, department name, or project details.

3. SMS Phishing (Smishing)

Fake mobile SMS claiming your government bank account or salary payment requires urgent link verification.

4. Phone Phishing (Vishing)

Fake phone calls from someone pretending to be from Ministry IT asking for your OTP or password.

Simple Action Checklist

Follow these 5 simple steps whenever an unexpected email lands in your inbox at work:

1. Check sender domain (@gov.lk) 2. Hover over links first 3. Call sender directly if unsure 4. Delete unverified emails 5. Inform Divisional IT Officer
