Weak Passwords (Common Danger)

Avoid simple words like gampaha123, admin@2025, or birth years. Automated hacker tools can guess these in under 2 seconds!

Strong Passphrases (Government Standard)

Use long passphrases combining uppercase, lowercase, numbers, and symbols (e.g. Gampaha@2026!Sec or Rainy#Gampaha$88). 12+ characters take centuries to crack.

3

Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA / 2FA)

Even if an attacker steals your password, MFA sends a 6-digit code or prompt to your official mobile phone. Without your phone, hackers are completely blocked!

4

Never Share Credentials

Never share your password or OTP code with anyone — not even coworkers or persons claiming to be IT support. Official IT officers will NEVER ask for your password!

✅ What Government Staff SHOULD Do

  • Use passphrases with 12+ characters for all e-government portals.
  • Enable 2FA / MFA on all work accounts and phones.
  • Use a unique password for work that is different from personal Facebook/email.

❌ What Government Staff Should NEVER Do

  • Never write passwords on sticky notes attached to your PC monitor or desk drawer.
  • Never click "Remember Password" on public or shared computers.
  • Never tell your OTP or login code to anyone over the phone or WhatsApp.
What Makes a Strong Password?

A strong password is your first line of defense against cybercriminals attempting to access government information systems.

Length (Most Important)

A password should be at least 12-14 characters long. A longer password is exponentially harder to crack.

Complexity

Include a mix of uppercase letters, lowercase letters, numbers, and special symbols (e.g., @, #, $, %).

Uniqueness

Never use the same password for multiple accounts. If one account is compromised, they all are.

Avoid Dictionary Words

Instead of "Password!23", use a passphrase like "BlueCoffeeCup!42"

Common Password Mistakes
  • Using sequences like "123456", "qwerty", or "password".
  • Using personal information (names, birthdates, pet names, ID numbers).
  • Reusing your personal social media password for government systems.
  • Writing passwords on sticky notes and leaving them on your monitor.
  • Changing only one number when forced to reset (e.g., P@ssword1 to P@ssword2).
Credential Stuffing

Cybercriminals use automated tools to test stolen passwords from public breaches on other websites. This is why password reuse is highly dangerous.

Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

MFA adds an extra layer of security. Even if a hacker steals your password, they cannot log in without the second factor.

It requires two or more of the following:

Something you know (Password/PIN) Something you have (Phone/Security Key) Something you are (Fingerprint/Face ID)
Password Managers & Government Policy

Memorizing dozens of unique, complex passwords is impossible. A password manager is a secure software application that stores and encrypts all your passwords.

Sri Lanka Government Employee Guidelines:

  • Always adhere to the Divisional Secretariat's IT policy regarding password resets.
  • Never share your login credentials with colleagues, even if they need temporary access.
  • Use the official enterprise password manager if provided by the IT department.
