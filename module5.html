Scammers sending WhatsApp messages pretending to be your Divisional Secretary asking you to buy voucher codes or transfer funds urgently. Verify face-to-face first!

✅ What Government Staff SHOULD Do

  • Independently verify callers using official internal extension directories.
  • Challenge unfamiliar visitors walking in secure office hallways without badges.
  • Hand any lost USB drives directly to your IT administrator without plugging them in.

❌ What Government Staff Should NEVER Do

  • Never disclose internal passwords, NIC details, or OTPs over phone calls or WhatsApp.
  • Never hold open secure server room doors for unverified visitors.
  • Never act on sudden financial/money transfer requests sent via messaging apps.
What is Social Engineering?

Social engineering is the art of manipulating people into performing actions or divulging confidential information. Unlike hacking, it targets human psychology rather than software. Government employees are prime targets because they have access to sensitive citizen data and public resources.

94% of Cyberattacks Start with Human Error

Most successful breaches don't involve cracking encryption — they involve tricking a person. Social engineering is the entry point for the majority of corporate and government data breaches worldwide.

Common Social Engineering Techniques

Pretexting

The attacker creates a fabricated scenario (a "pretext") to extract information. E.g., "I'm from the IT team and need your password to fix an urgent issue."

Impersonation

Pretending to be a trusted person — a colleague, IT support, a vendor, or a government auditor — to gain access or information.

Baiting

Leaving an infected USB drive or offering a "free download" to lure victims into infecting their own systems out of curiosity or greed.

Tailgating / Piggybacking

Physically following an authorized person into a restricted area (e.g., a server room) without their own access card.

Quid Pro Quo

Offering a service (e.g., "free IT support") in exchange for information or access credentials.

Vishing (Voice Phishing)

A phone call from a fake "bank" or "government authority" demanding urgent action or personal verification.

Psychological Triggers Used by Attackers
  • Authority: "This is the Director-General's office. You must act immediately."
  • Urgency: "If you don't do this in the next 30 minutes, the system will shut down."
  • Scarcity/Fear: "Your account will be permanently deleted unless you verify now."
  • Reciprocity: "I helped you with that report last month, so you can just give me temporary access."
  • Social Proof: "Everyone else in your department has already done this."
